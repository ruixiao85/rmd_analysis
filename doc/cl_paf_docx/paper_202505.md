**Abstract**

**Backgrounds:** Approximately 20-30% of paroxysmal atrial fibrillation (PAF) may progress to heart failure (HF). Further studies are warranted to elucidate the specific characteristics of PAF that contribute to HF development. We evaluated whether early cardiac dysfunction markers, noninvasive left ventricular myocardial work (LVMW) and left atrial (LA) remodeling, could predict HF incident.

**Methods:** A cross-sectional study and a prospective cohort study were conducted to evaluate early cardiac dysfunction in PAF using LVMW and LA remodeling parameters. AF burden (AFB) and its progression were assessed through standardized questionnaires. The primary outcome was incident HF. Multivariable logistic regression adjusted by confounding was used to determine the odds ratios of these parameters for HF incident, with statistical analyses performed by Stata 15.0 and R 4.1.

**Results:** We enrolled 146 PAF and 63 non-PAF subjects set as the controls in the study. The average age was 66.2±11.4 years and comprised 55% males in PAF. Compared with the controls, PAF had significantly elevated global wasted work (GWW) (143.7±88.3mmHg% vs 115.5±59.6mmHg%, p<0.001) and impaired global work efficiency (GWE) (92.3±7.5% vs 93.8±2.8%, p=0.035), correlated with increased LA volume index max (LAVImax) and LA volume index minimal (LAVImin). Higher AFB showed significantly reduced LA ejection fraction (LAEF) and increased LAVImin. During the follow-up of 40.5 months, 9.9% PAF developed HF, and ablation reduced HF incidence. In the non-ablation subgroup, reduced baseline LAEF <45% independently predicted subsequent HF development.

**Conclusions:** Elevated GWW was strongly correlated with LA dilation in PAF, while higher AFB was associated with reduced LAEF and increased LAVImin. Early restoring sinus rhythm in PAF patients with LAEF below 45% should be proposed strongly for HF prevention.

**Key words:** Paroxysmal Atrial Fibrillation, Noninvasive Left Ventricle Myocardial Work, Left Atrial Remodeling, Atrial Fibrillation Burden, Heart Failure Incident

**Introduction**

The prevention of stroke and systemic embolism in atrial fibrillation (AF) has been well-established by extensive clinical evidence in recent years^1^. However, the detection and prevention for heart failure (HF) in AF remain to be discovered. Recent cohort in Denmark revealed that patients with AF faced twice the risk of developing HF compared to stroke^2^. Notably, when AF coexists with HF, the risk of cardiovascular mortality may increase threefold compared to AF patients without HF^3^. Although sinus rhythm restoration by ablation has proven effective in reducing HF events in AF, its clinical implementation remained limited. In this condition, research should be applicated to identify the specific features of AF that contribute to HF progression, thereby providing stronger evidence-based recommendations for sinus rhythm restoration in this subtype population. A more comprehensive assessment of subclinical left ventricle (LV) and left atrial (LA) dysfunction in paroxysmal atrial fibrillation (PAF) may yield pivotal insights into this pathophysiology.

We here introduced the noninvasive left ventricle myocardial work (LVMW)^4^ and LA remodeling to identify the early cardiac dysfunction in PAF. LVMW index, including LV global work index (GWI), LV global constructive work (GCW), LV global wasted work (GWW) and LV global work efficiency (GWE), can be obtained from LV pressure-strain loop analysis incorporating peripheral arterial blood pressure and LV global longitudinal strain (GLS) deriving from the two-dimensional speckle tracking echocardiography. Compared with left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF), GLS offers more sensitive assessment of LV function across various HF phenotypes and subclinical LV impairment^5,\ 6^. Moreover, LVMW provides more comprehensive evaluation of LV performance throughout the entire cardiac cycle and allows earlier identification of LV dysfunction. In addition, LA plays a critical role in cardiac performance in AF. Our previous work has already identified distinct patterns of LA remodeling and dysfunction using three-dimensional method in PAF^7^. The interaction between LA remodeling and LVMW index warrants investigation. On the other hand, recent guidelines emphasize the importance of assessing AF burden (AFB) in PAF, which may provide important prognosis information for PAF. LA remodeling has been linked to AFB^8^, while the association between LVMW and AFB remains unclear.

We proposed a cross-sectional study and a prospective cohort study to address these gaps: 1) To evaluate the utility of LVMW and LA remolding in detecting early cardiac dysfunction in PAF. 2) To examine the correlation between LVMW parameters and LA remodeling. 3) To investigate the association among LVMW, LA remodeling, and AFB. 4) To investigate subclinical cardiac dysfunction as a predictor of subsequent HF incident.

**Methods**

**Ethical statements**

This research complies with the guidelines for human studies according to Helsinki Declaration. The study protocol had been approved by the institute's committee on human research from Huashan Hospital, Fudan University (2020-788).

**Study design and population**

This study was comprised of a cross-sectional study and a prospective cohort study in our single center. In the cross-sectional study, we enrolled patients with PAF diagnosed by 12-lead electrocardiogram (ECG) or 24-hour Holter monitoring and age- and gender-matched controls without AF or major cardiovascular diseases in Huashan Hospital between March 2019--December 2019 and September 2020--March 2021 (with enrollment interruptions due to the COVID-19 pandemic). All participants underwent assessment of LVMW and LA remodeling parameters. Moderate-to-severe valvular stenosis/regurgitation (mitral, tricuspid, or aortic), HF of any stage, acute myocardial infarction (≤6 months), and acute pulmonary embolism (≤3 months) were excluded. Baseline clinical data, including demographic characteristics, current medications, and cardiovascular history, were obtained through structured review of electronic medical records and standardized questionnaires.

In the prospective study, the primary endpoint was HF incident at the end of the follow up. The definition of HF incident included subjects who experienced symptoms such as shortness of breath, fatigue and nocturnal sitting breathing with NT-pro BNP elevation greater than 300pg/ml in emergency room or outpatient department evidenced by medical records.

**AFB evaluation by questionnaire**

A questionnaire including 5 questions, cited from previous researches was used to evaluate the AFB in our study and the questionnaire was finished guiding by the researcher on each subject (Table S2). Additionally, AFB questionnaire would be used again to evaluate the AFB of enrolled PAF at the end of the follow up. AFB deterioration was defined as the AFB group changed from the minimal group to mild-severe group or to the persistent AF, or the AFB group changed from the mild-severe group to the persistent AF.

**Echocardiography**

**Conventional LV examination**

Comprehensive transthoracic echocardiography (TTE) was performed by technician using the GE Vivid E95 system (Horten, Norway) with 4Vc-D 4D (1.5-4.0 MHz) matrix cardiac probes. Two-dimensional echocardiography (2DE), color Doppler flow imaging, continuous- and pulsed-wave Doppler spectrum, and tissue Doppler image (TDI) on the bilateral mitral annulus were performed in accordance with the current American Society of Echocardiography guidelines. The system settings were optimized to ensure the best image quality with the 2DE image frame rate ranged in 50-70 frames/sec. The loops of 3-4 cardiac cycles were acquired for every standard view and saved digitally for further analysis. Left ventricular end-diastolic diameter (LVDD), left ventricular end-systolic diameter (LVSD), interventricular septum (IVSd) and left ventricular posterior wall(LVPWd) thickness, left ventricular mass index (LVMI) were calculated with M mode methods. Left ventricular end-diastolic volume (LVEDV), left ventricular end-systolic volume (LVESV), left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) were calculated with bi-plane Simpson's methods. The mitral valve inflow map was recorded by pulsed-wave Doppler echocardiography to obtain early (E) and late (A) diastolic inflow velocity, E to A ratio and deceleration time (DT) of E wave. The peak early (e') and late (a') diastolic mitral annular velocities TDI at both septal and lateral sides of mitral ring, and the average E/e' were calculated to assess the LV filling pressure.

**LVMW evaluation**

LV GLS was analyzed by an echocardiography work station (EchoPAC 203, General Electric Healthcare, Horten, Norway). With the AFI option, the station would automatically detect the LV endocardial border and depict LV regional speckle tracking on the three 2D apical long-axis views respectively. Manual correction should be done for inaccurate endocardial borders' delineation. Appropriately selected ROI according to the myocardial thickness would be used to obtain accurate speckle tracking of each view. LV GLS would be calculated as the average of the peak regional systolic longitudinal strain values of the 17 LV segments. Patients were excluded if the endocardial border delineation was not ideally tracked for one or more segments. Afterwards, LVMW parameters would be calculated automatically according to the GLS data and blood pressure recordings. The peak systolic LV pressure was assumed equal to peak arterial pressure. Right brachial cuff blood pressure would be measured simultaneously by electronic sphygmomanometer at 5-min resting sitting-position just before the TTE examination. LVMW parameters^9^ including GCW, GWW, GWI, GWE depicted in table and figures (Table S1, Figure 1).

**LA remodeling assessment**

LA diameters in suprainferior, mediolateral and anteroposterior directions were measured at the end of LV systole by 2D methods. The full volume image of LA was acquired by 3-dimensional echocardiography (3DE) on the apical 4-chamber view with the cardiac probe. Care must be taken during one-beat acquisition to ensure that the complete LA is included, and that the volume rate exceeds 12 volumes per second for adequate temporal assessment. LA volume index (LAVI) was analyzed using the 4-dimensional automated LA quantification (4D Auto LAQ) option of the work station, which would automatically track the LA border and calculate the real time LV volume change during the cardiac cycle. If tracking is not available, automatic tracking of atrial boundary at times and manual adjustment would be done. The results provided an overview of LA remodeling in terms of the various parameters, which were calculated and rendered as data report by the software, and also as the LA volume-time curve:
- LA Vmin = Minimum atrial volume
- LA VImin = Minimum atrial volume/calculated body surface area
- LA Vmax = Maximum atrial volume
- LA VImax = Maximum atrial volume /calculated body surface area
- LA VpreA = Volume at onset of atrial contraction
- LA EV = Ejection Volume (LA Vmax - LA Vmin)
- LA EF = Ejection fraction (LA EV / LA Vmax)

**Statistical analysis**

Normally distributed continuous variables were presented as mean±SD, whereas non-normally distributed data were expressed as median and interquartile range. Categorical variables were expressed as percentages. T-test was used for comparison between the PAF group and the control or in subgroups analysis if the data were normally continuous distributed. Chi-square test or Fisher's exact test as appropriate was performed in categorical data comparison. The correlation between LVMW and LA parameters were performed using the Pearson coefficient method if data were normally distributed or the Spearman coefficient if the data were skewed. The same operator as well as a different observer repeat the offline analysis in three months after the initial measurements. Logistic regression analysis was used to reveal the odds ratio of LVMW and LA remodeling for HF incident. Age, gender, hypertension, diabetes, coronary artery disease and ablation therapy were set as the confounding in the multiple logistic regression model. In order to look for a cutoff point for LAEF prediction for HF incidence, "Cut point" package was used. This method estimated cut points that optimized a specified metric in binary classification tasks and validate performance using bootstrapping. At last, 60 subjects from the PAF group and 30 datasets from the control group were selected to assess the operation reproducibly. The intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) was used to assess reproducibility. Stata 15.0 and R 4.1 were used for statistical analysis and p value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

**Results**

**PART I-Cross-sectional study**

**Baseline characteristics of enrolled PAF and the controls subjects**

In the study, 169 PAF patients were enrolled and 23 of them were excluded because of valvular disease (n=12), HF (n=3), recent myocardial infarction (n=3) and low segment tracking quality (n=5). 146 PAF patients were finally included in the analysis and 38 males and 25 females were matched as the controls for baseline analysis (Table 1). There were no statistically differences in terms of age, gender, BMI and diastolic blood pressure. Systolic blood pressure was statistically higher in the PAF group compared with it in the control group. The history of hypertension, diabetes, coronary artery disease, stroke and peripheral vascular disease were more prevalent in the PAF group.

**LV structure & function and LA remodeling of enrolled PAF and the
controls subjects**

The interventricular septum and LV posterior wall were thicker in PAF compared with the controls, while the LVMI between two groups was nearly consistent. LVEDV and LVESV were significantly larger in PAF than the controls. Compared with the control group, the PAF group had higher mitral E velocity and statistically increased E/A ratio. Whereas, E/e' was significantly higher in the PAF group. LVEF and GLS remained nearly consistent in the two groups. The peak strain dispersion (PSD) significantly increased in the PAF group, indicating impaired LV synchrony in PAF group. Diastolic dysfunction degree 1-3 were more prevalent in PAF compared with the controls (Table 2). LA was significantly dilated in PAF group and the 3-DE measurements of the maximal and minimal LA volume and LAVI were all significantly larger than those in the control group. Additionally, significantly reduced LAEF was observed in PAF group (Table 2).

**Elevated GWW in PAF compared with the controls**

Compared with the control group, GCW and GWI in the PAF group showed slightly reduced trend with no statistical significance (2244.4±659.8 mmHg% vs 2237.5±473.1 mmHg%, p=0.94; 2021.1±400.7 mmHg% vs 2003.3±403.6 mmHg%, p=0.77). GWW was significantly elevated in the PAF group compared to the controls (115.5±59.6 mmHg% vs 143.7±88.3 mmHg%, p=0.0065) and GWE was significantly reduced in PAF (93.8±2.8% vs 92.3±7.5%, p=0.0035) (Figure2 and Table S3).

In the subgroups analysis of PAF, significantly elevated GWW (157.3±95.4 mm Hg% vs 123.6±74.5 mm Hg%, p=0.04) and impaired GWE (92.3±2.6% vs 93.6±3.7%, p=0.04) were showed in the hypertension group compared with the non-hypertension group (Table S4). There were no significant differences of LVMW in the subgroups of age dichotomous, gender and type 2 diabetes.

**Significant correlation between elevated GWW and LAVImax or LAVImin in PAF**

Given the intimae physiological interplay between LV function and LA mechanics in PAF, we investigated the association between LVMW and LA remodeling. Significant correlations were found between: (1) GWW and both LAVImax (r=0.21, p=0.014) and LAVImin (r=0.19, p=0.023); and (2) GCW and LAVImax (r=0.23, p<0.01). A weaker but significant association was observed between GWI and LAVImax (r=0.18, p=0.034). Notably, no significant correlations were discovered between LVMW and LAEF (Figure 3 and Table S5).

**Reduced LAEF and increase LAVImin were correlated with severe AFB in PAF**

To investigate the potential impact of AFB on LVMW and LA remodeling in PAF, we analyzed 133 subjects who completed the questionnaire (with 17 excluded due to comprehension difficulties). 133 PAF were classified into minimal group (n=51, 38.3%) and mild-severe group (n=82, 61.7%). Compared with the minimal group, significantly reduced LAEF (41.1±9.2 vs 46.8±9.6, p=0.004) and significantly increased LAVImin (17.6±5.7vs15.4±5.6, p=0.049) were found in mild-severe group. No significant differences were observed in LVMW parameters between AFB groups (Table 3).

**PART II-Prospective Cohort study**

**LAEF below 45% significantly correlated with HF incident in PAF**

At the average follow-up of 40.5 months, 111 patients (76.03%) have finished the follow-up by telephone or at outpatient department, while 35 patients were lost to follow up because of no response to telephone or telephone number changing. 41(36.9%) of them received catheter ablation after enrollment and 5 subjects received ablation during the follow-up because of the AFB deterioration.

Eleven patients (9.9%) developed into heart failure, including one (2.7%) of whom received ablation and (13.7%) ten of whom didn't (table S6). It also showed that AFB deterioration was significantly associated with HF incident (p<0.01) (table S7).Lower LAEF(OR:0.92, 95%CI 0.82-0.99, p=0.067), rather than LVMW parameters, showed greater risk of developing into HF after adjusting for age, gender, hypertension, diabetes, CAD and ablation (Figure 4-1).Increased baseline LAVImin have the potential values to predict AFB deterioration (OR=1.11, p=0.02, 95%CI 1.02-1.23) (Figure S1).

Additionally, we were interested in investigating the correlation between LVMW, LA remodeling and HF incident in subgroup of whom didn't receive the catheter ablation, to figure out the characteristics of PAF that may prone to developing into HF. Baseline lower LAEF revealed positive correlation with HF incident in this subgroup (OR=0.92, 95%CI 0.83-0.99, p=0.065) (Figure 4-2).The cutoff point of LAEF below 45% maximized the sum of sensitivity and specificity, as shown in the ROC curve, to predict HF incident (Figure 5).

**Reproducibility of GLS and LVMW parameters**

60 PAF subjects and 30 controls were selected randomly to assess the reproducibility of the GLS and LVMW in terms of the interobserver and intraobserver variability. No significant differences were found in interobserver and intraobserver variability analysis (Table S8). The reproducibility of LA parameters was compared in our previous study^7^. 
**Discussions**

The study was comprised of cross-sectional study and cohort study to utilized LVMW and LA remodeling to provide a comprehensive depiction of cardiac dysfunction in PAF, providing valuable information for recognizing the characteristics of PAF that are prone to developing into HF. First, we discovered that the elevated GWW is one of the major changes in PAF and was significantly correlated with enlarged LAVImin and LAVImax. Second, increased LAVImin as well as lower LAEF was associated with higher AFB. Third, the overall HF incidence was 9.9% during 40.5 months follow up and PAF patients with LAEF below 45% were at increased risk of developing HF.

**Elevated GWW accompanied with preserved GCW in PAF**

Despite comparable GLS and LVEF, PAF patients demonstrated higher GWW and preserved GCW versus controls. Noninvasive GWW could be viewed as a method to evaluate the synchronization performance in LV, similar to PSD^10^. As analyzed, PSD remained the same trend as GWW. It was reported that the GWW coincides with GCW in myocardium for accomplishing excitation-contraction coupling, overcoming myocardial resistance and maintaining ventricle tension, respectively^11^. In this scenario, GWW demonstrated the presence of negative mechanical work in PAF, reflecting both increased LV pressure and inefficient force expenditure by LV segments. GCW is mainly affected by myocardium strength, afterload and preload of LV^5^. No significant differences were found in GCW between two groups, indicating reserved stroke work in PAF. Recent evidence highlights that LVMW is valuable for investigating LV function in various cardiovascular diseases. Skulstad et al revealed LVMW was superior to LVEF as well as GLS in identifying patients with acute coronary occlusion in non-ST segment elevation myocardial infarction^12^. In cardiac amyloidosis, LVMW was also used to predict the prognosis^6^. Our data compensated the missing studies in AF area, which was one of the very first attempts to take advantage of LVMW to evaluate LV function in PAF.

Moreover, hypertensive subjects in PAF exhibited impaired LVMW compared to normotensive individuals. Consequently, GWE, which quantifies the ratio of effective LV work per minute, was also significantly reduced in hypertensive PAF patients^13^.We then hypothesis that increased LA volume and asynchronism of LV may contribute to these changes. The study of larger sample size in the future could be implemented to figure out the underlying mechanism.

**The significant correlation between elevated GWW and enlarged LA volume index**

The study examined the relationship between LVMW and LA remodeling. Increased GWW was significantly correlated with larger LAVImin and LAVImax, while diminished GWI and GWE were strongly associated with increased LAVImax.

LA plays important role in LV performance in AF^14^. Our previous study demonstrated significant LA dysfunction and enlargement in AF, characterized by progressive structural remodeling, subtle LA volume expansion, and subclinical functional impairment^7^. Notably, our analysis positioned both LAVImin and LAVImax as central mediators in the relationship with LVMW. Existing research has consistently linked LAVImax to LV functional assessment in various conditions. In contrast, LAVImin has been less studied, with the exception of its robust correlation with increased LV filling pressures in myocardial infarction, leading to calls for its concurrent measurement with LAVImax.^15^. While in our study, elevated GWW was strongly associated with increased LV end-diastolic pressure, indicating that LAVImin might reflect physiologically meaningful changes related to LV mechanical inefficiency.

**Higher AFB significantly associated with reduced LAEF and enlarged LAVImin**

Accumulating data demonstrated that lowering AFB by catheter ablation or medication turns out to have better clinical outcomes than rate control in AF ^12,\ 16,\ 17^. CASTLE-AF study^18^ showed that, in persistent-AF with LV dysfunction, there was a significantly reduced risk of worsening heart failure in the catheter ablation group compared to the conventional treatment group. AFB had also been used to guide intermittent anticoagulation in PAF^19^. However, the impact of AFB on cardiac function in PAF without pre-existing HF has been insufficiently explored. Our work addresses this critical knowledge gap through comprehensive assessment.

Significantly reduced LAEF and enlarged LAVImin and a trend of elevated GWW and impaired GWE with non-significance were also found in mild-severe AFB group. The questionnaire we used to assess the AFB in this study was proved consistent with the impaired LA function in our previous work^7^.Solomon et al proved that LAVImin was more predictive of cardiovascular death and HF hospitalization in HF with preserved ejection fraction^20^. In patients with myocardial infarction, both enlarged LAVImin and LAVImax were independent predictors of MACE, especially for LAVImin^15^. LAVImin rather than LAVImax was found to strongly predict the incident HF or death in the Atherosclerosis Risk in Communities (ARIC) cohort^21^.

Our findings demonstrated a significant association between LA remodeling and increased AFB in PAF. However, AFB showed no direct effect on GWW or other LVMW parameters, suggesting these relationships may involve more complex pathophysiology requiring further investigation.

**LAEF below 45% maybe a key indicator for predicting HF**

During a mean follow-up of 40.5 months, 9.9% of PAF progressed to heart failure (HF), with significantly different rates between subgroups (13.7% in non-ablated vs 2.7% in ablated patients). Subgroup analysis of non-ablated patients revealed that baseline LAEF, but not LVMW parameters, independently predicted HF development. Notably, PAF patients with LAEF <45% demonstrated substantially higher HF incident. This finding, which was equivalent to the discovery that AFB was significantly correlated with reduced LAEF, provides further evidence that AFB contributes to HF development. In EAST-AFNET 4 trial, rhythm control in early AF showed lower risk of developing into worsening HF compared to the rate control group^22^. The EARLY-AF trial demonstrated that in PAF, early ablation reduced the incidence of persistent AF or recurrent atrial tachyarrhythmia compared with antiarrhythmic medication^23^.Additionally, the subgroup analysis in CASTLE-AF revealed that higher AFB rather than AF reoccurrence after catheter ablation or medication treatment was referred to higher risk of all-cause mortality and worsening HF in AF concurrent with HF^24^. However, AFB evaluation is time-consuming and lacks accuracy. Our findings indicated that that PAF patients with an LAEF below 45% served as both a reliable marker for assessing AFB and a valuable predictor for identifying high-risk individuals requiring early HF prevention strategies like ablation.

The result of the part of prospective cohort study supplemented the data in PAF for HF prevention. Therefore, in PAF patients with LAEF <45%, early implementation of rhythm control strategies or AF burden reduction should be prioritized to prevent both HF development and AF progression. 
**Limitations**

This single center prospective study had some limitations. First of all, small sample size of the study and missing data in the follow up may weaken the power of the conclusion. Secondly, AFB assessment by questionnaire may have the problem of recall bias due to the sophisticated questionnaire design as well as time consuming distinction.

**Conclusions**

Elevated GWW with impaired GWE and preserved GCW were found in PAF and elevated GWW was strongly correlated with increased LA volume index. Increased LAVImin and reduced LAEF rather than LVMW was found strongly correlated with higher AFB. Early maintaining sinus rhythm or minimizing the AFB demonstrated significant value for HF prevention in PAF with LAEF below 45%.

| Parameters               | Definitions                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |

| LV Global Constructive Work |                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| **GWW(mmHg%)** | Work performed by the LV that does not contribute to LV ejection. GWW performed as lengthening of myocytes (rather than shortening) during systole, adding shortening during the isovolumic relaxation phase.                                          |
| LV Global Wasted Work    |                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| **GWI(mmHg%)** | The average MW of the LV pressure-strain loop (the three apical chambers), calculated from mitral valve closure to mitral valve opening.                                                                                                            |
| LV Global Work Index     |                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| **GWE(%)** | Expressed as a percentage which is GCW divided by the sum of GCW and GWW (these values will not be affected by peak LV pressure).                                                                                                                     |
| LV global work efficiency |                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |


### Supplementary Table 2: Questionnaire for AFB

1.  **Frequency of AF episodes based on symptoms (e.g., palpitation, irregularly irregular pulse, fatigue, etc.)**
    * None in the recent three months (jump to question 3): **0 point**
    * About once to four times per year: **1 point**
    * About once or twice per month: **2 points**
    * About once per week: **3 points**
    * More than twice per week: **4 points**
    * Daily: **5 points**
2.  **Duration of each AF episode**
    * Less than 60 minutes: **1 point**
    * About a few hours: **2 points**
    * Lasting most of the day: **3 points**
    * More than one day: **4 points**
3.  **Times of visiting the emergency room due to AF in the last five years**
    * 0: **0 point**
    * 1: **1 point**
    * 2: **2 points**
    * 3: **3 points**
    * >4: **4 points**
4.  **Times of receiving electroversion in the last five years**
    * 0: **0 point**
    * 1: **1 point**
    * 2: **2 points**
    * 3: **3 points**
    * >4: **4 points**
5.  **Times of hospitalization due to AF (not including hospitalization for elective electroversion)**
    * 0: **0 point**
    * 1: **1 point**
    * 2: **2 points**
    * 3: **3 points**
    * >4: **4 points**

**Total score = sum of all the five questions**
* 0: asymptomatic
* 1-3: minimal AFB
* 4-6: mild AFB
* 7-9: moderate AFB
* >9: severe AFB
